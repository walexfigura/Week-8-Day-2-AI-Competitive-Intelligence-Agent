# Signal prioritization criteria

Assess each candidate signal on six factors and explain the result in one or two sentences.

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Business relevance | Does it affect a variant that directly competes with one of our products? |
| Magnitude | How big is the computed change? (≥10% price = large) |
| Evidence quality | Comparable, complete, primary-source observations? |
| Customer impact | Do reviews or segments suggest customers will notice? |
| Urgency | Does waiting a week cost anything? |
| Confidence | Direct observation vs interpretation vs partial data |

## Tiers
- **High** — act this week. Example: 17.9% price decrease on the direct competitor to the
  best seller; a leak-proof lid added by the direct competitor of a product whose lid is
  not leak-proof.
- **Medium** — investigate soon. Example: a premium brand repositions toward commuters;
  a partially comparable product sells out.
- **Low** — record only.
- **Insufficient evidence** — cannot conclude (variant/currency mismatch, partial page, rumor).
- **No alert** — formatting-only change; promotion ended as stated. Saying "this does
  not warrant an alert" is a valuable output.

The tiers are a transparent judgment, not a measured business impact. Say so if you
show a score.

## Allowed recommendations (for humans)
Review pricing on an affected product · investigate a new competitor feature · check our
product-page positioning · examine a recurring complaint theme · collect more
observations to verify a change.

## Never do automatically
Change prices · edit product descriptions · publish marketing content · contact
competitors · post to social media · send alerts or emails without explicit approval.
